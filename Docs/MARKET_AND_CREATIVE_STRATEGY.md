# Simon Duel — Market and Creative Strategy

**Status:** Pre-production decision brief  
**Research snapshot:** 20 August 2026  
**Working title:** Simon Duel — internal codename pending legal/name clearance  
**Purpose:** Convert current market, competitor, player-experience, art, VFX, audio, haptic, accessibility, and business-model research into a focused first playable.

## 1. Executive decision

Do not build or market another generic Simon clone or a brain-training utility.

Build a **local-first pocket party duel** in which two players create one growing pattern for each other, spend limited tactical Twists, hand over the same phone, and immediately rematch.

> **Recommended product promise:** Build the pattern. Break your friend.

The defensible experience is not simply “multiplayer Simon.” Existing products already offer online races, Daily Challenges, Reverse, Shuffle, themes, haptics, leaderboards, and multiple board layouts. The opportunity is the combination of:

- zero-friction, one-phone social play;
- player-authored challenge rather than a machine-authored test;
- limited, clearly telegraphed tactical interference;
- readable social theater for the opponent and nearby spectators;
- tactile musical feedback polished to rhythm-game standards;
- short matches with an immediate rematch and persistent local rivalry.

### First-playable thesis

Two players should be able to start in under 20 seconds, understand the full normal turn after seeing it once, experience a dramatic decision within the first minute, finish in 60–180 seconds, and choose Rematch without being prompted by a reward system.

### What to prove before adding scope

1. Does choosing the next note feel like an attack rather than admin?
2. Do players understand a Twist before input begins?
3. Does the handoff create anticipation, banter, blame, or laughter?
4. Does a finished match naturally produce a rematch?
5. Can players follow the board by shape, tone, or haptic without relying on color alone?

## 2. Market reality

Mobile is large but mature. Sensor Tower reported roughly **$82 billion in mobile-game IAP revenue and 52 billion downloads in 2025**. Growth increasingly comes from engagement, retention, live operations, and stronger monetization mixes rather than a flood of new downloads. Puzzle remained unusually resilient in Q2 2026, with reported IAP growth and positive download growth while the wider games market softened.

The practical implication is simple: a familiar mechanic is useful for comprehension, but it is not a reason to install or return. The game needs a clear social story in store media and a satisfying first 90 seconds.

GameAnalytics' 2026 report, based on 16,000+ mobile games with 1,000+ MAU during 2025, reports:

| Metric | Median mobile game | Top-quartile reference | Product implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Session length | 3.1–3.5 min | about 5.2 min | A 2–3 minute duel fits the natural window. |
| Daily playtime | about 12 min | 22–24 min | Design for several short matches, not one long session. |
| Sessions per day | 3.8–3.9 | 5.3–5.7 | A rematch loop matters more than content-heavy navigation. |
| Day-1 retention | about 22% | just above 30% | The first session must reach the social payoff immediately. |
| Day-7 retention | just under 4% | 6–7% | Local rivalry, daily seeds, and light collection may support return. |
| Day-30 retention | 0.68–0.79% | 1.6–1.8% | Do not assume longevity; instrument and earn it. |

Sources: [Sensor Tower 2026 State of Gaming press release](https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-gaming-drove-52-billion-downloads-82b-iap-revenue-on-mobile-and-12b-premium-revenue-on-steam), [Sensor Tower Q2 2026 Digital Market Index](https://sensortower.com/blog/q2-2026-digital-market-index-report), [GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks).

### 3.3 Brain-training and rhythm adjacencies

Brain-training leaders such as [Lumosity](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lumoslabs.lumosity), [Peak](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brainbow.peak.app), and [Impulse](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/impulse-brain-training/id1451295827) show that named daily activities, personal baselines, streaks, and short workouts can create return reasons. They also carry content-library and subscription expectations this project should not inherit.

Rhythm products such as [Magic Tiles 3](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.youmusic.magictiles), [Beatstar](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.spaceapegames.beatstar), and [A Dance of Fire and Ice](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fizzd.connectedworlds) demonstrate that a simple input can feel premium when audio, visuals, latency, and touch response are one system. Reviews of ad-heavy rhythm games also reinforce that advertising must never break a rally, handoff, or immediate rematch.

Do not claim medical, academic, intelligence, or broad real-world cognitive benefits. Position the product as a memory game or pattern-recall challenge. The [FTC's Lumosity settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program) and a [working-memory training meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4968033/) support a conservative claims policy.

## 4. White-space map

The useful market gap sits between four established poles:

| Pole | Typical strength | Typical weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Classic Simon clone | Immediate comprehension and nostalgia | Little agency, novelty, or social identity |
| Brain-training utility | Habit systems and performance history | Clinical tone, subscriptions, high content burden |
| Local minigame collection | Frictionless social play and variety | Shallow identity per game and menu sprawl |
| Multiplayer party spectacle | Strong human reactions | Networking/device setup and comprehension friction |

Simon Duel should occupy the center as a **single, authored social toy**: easier to start than a networked party game, more expressive than a clone, less clinical than brain training, and more ownable than a minigame inside a collection.

### Positioning statement

For friends, couples, siblings, and families who want a fast contest anywhere, Simon Duel is a one-phone tactical memory duel where each player builds the pattern and can bend the next turn with one readable Twist. Unlike solo memory clones or connection-heavy multiplayer, it creates face-to-face rivalry in seconds.

### Store-facing proof sequence

1. **Build the pattern** — show Player 1 repeating and choosing the final note.
2. **Twist the rules** — reveal Shift or Reverse before Player 2 watches.
3. **Break your friend** — show the mistake, reaction, point, and Rematch.

The store trailer should communicate the loop with hands and reactions, not feature-list text.

## 5. Audience and play occasions

### Primary audience

- friends, siblings, couples, parents and children;
- broad casual players aged approximately 8+;
- people sharing a couch, table, queue, commute, break, trip, or family gathering;
- players willing to learn one rule but not a deck-building metagame.

### Core jobs to be done

- “Give us something fun in the next two minutes.”
- “Let me challenge this person without accounts or setup.”
- “Give us a rematchable rivalry where a loss feels like my mistake.”
- “Make a tiny phone game feel satisfying enough to show another person.”

### Session shape

- Open to first interactive turn: **under 20 seconds**.
- First normal turn taught: **under 35 seconds**.
- First Twist encountered: **45–75 seconds**, after both players understand normal play.
- Match: **60–180 seconds** for the first playable; consider best-of-five only after testing.
- Rematch to gameplay: **under 5 seconds**.

## 6. Product pillars

1. **Immediate social play** — no account, invite, network, lobby, or content download before local play.
2. **Authored tension** — every successful player chooses the next step, creating responsibility and intent.
3. **Readable tactics** — a Twist changes one mental rule, is named before playback, and never hides an interface failure.
4. **Tactile musicality** — every pad press produces an exact visual, sonic, and haptic consequence.
5. **Spectator clarity** — turn ownership, Twist state, sequence phase, mistake, and winner are readable from arm's length.
6. **Rematch velocity** — results celebrate briefly, preserve rivalry, and make Rematch dominant.

## 7. Recommended first playable

### Included

- one-device Pass & Play;
- best-of-three rally points;
- one shared sequence seeded with two steps;
- repeat, then add one player-selected step;
- three Twists: Reverse, Shift, Echo;
- one held Twist maximum; no stacking;
- simple bot fallback;
- instant rematch and local rivalry score;
- tutorial through play;
- separate music, SFX, and haptic controls;
- high-contrast, reduced-motion, and color-independent cues;
- instrumentation for comprehension, rally depth, mistakes, Twists, match completion, and rematch.

### Deferred

- real-time online matchmaking;
- accounts, chat, guilds, seasons, and ranked ladders;
- large card collections or deck construction;
- subscription;
- licensed songs;
- energy, paid continues, gameplay power, or rewarded ads inside the duel;
- more than three launch Twists;
- complex progression before the core social loop retains players.

## 8. Core loop and rules

`WATCH → REPEAT → ADD ONE → optionally ARM A TWIST → HAND OFF`

1. The active player and ownership color become prominent.
2. If a Twist is active, its name and one-sentence rule appear before playback.
3. The sequence plays with input locked.
4. A short Ready beat separates playback from input.
5. The player repeats the complete sequence.
6. On success, the state changes clearly to ADD ONE.
7. The player chooses one additional pad identity.
8. If charged, the player may arm one Twist for the opponent.
9. The phone is handed over and ownership changes.
10. A mistake or timeout ends the rally; the opponent scores.

Pad identity is always the complete bundle of **shape + color + tone + haptic signature**. If positions move, the entire identity moves.

## 9. Twist strategy

Twists should feel like tactical punctuation, not constant noise.

| Twist | Rule | Strategic purpose | Readability rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse | Enter the sequence from last step to first. | High-comprehension mental reversal. | Counter-clockwise perimeter travel and reverse earcon before playback. |
| Shift | Pad positions rotate after playback; enter by identity. | Breaks location memory without hiding information. | Entire color/shape/tone identities visibly move; movement completes before input. |
| Echo | One clearly marked step must be entered twice. | Creates a local disruption without rewriting the whole sequence. | The affected step uses a paired pulse and doubled beat marker. |

Use **Shift**, not generic Shuffle, in public language. Reverse and Shuffle already appear in competitors and historical Simon products; the unique value comes from when and why a player deploys a Twist.

### Twist economy baseline

- Twists disabled for the first two successful turns of a rally.
- One Charge per successful turn; Twist available at three Charge.
- One held Twist maximum.
- Arm only after successfully adding a step.
- Affect only the opponent's next turn.
- One Twist maximum per turn; never stack during the first playable.
- Controlled bag prevents repetitive draws.
- First encounter gets a demonstration; later encounters use a short banner.

Test an even simpler alternative: each player receives one visible Twist token per rally. Prefer the version that produces deliberate timing with less UI explanation.

## 10. Art direction

The approved visual direction should remain **tactile, luminous, friendly, focused, competitive, and premium casual**.

It occupies useful whitespace between flat, generic sequence clones and the pale, clinical style of brain-training products.

### Keep

- dark navy stage with peripheral UI dimming during memory phases;
- chunky 2.5D pad objects with clear silhouettes and physical depression;
- permanent coral Circle, sun-yellow Triangle, mint Square, and electric-blue Star identities;
- orange Player 1 and cyan Player 2 ownership accents;
- bold state language: WATCH, REPEAT, ADD ONE, REVERSE, SHIFT, ECHO;
- glass and neon as restrained framing, not a constant bloom layer;
- the approved references in [ART_BIBLE.md](ART_BIBLE.md).

### Avoid

- the classic circular four-quadrant Simon trade dress;
- a logo or store page that implies Hasbro affiliation;
- constant rainbow light or particles during recall;
- tiny card copy or weak state contrast;
- clinical brains, IQ scores, health dashboards, or school imagery;
- recoloring pads for success/error, which breaks identity;
- detailed backgrounds that compete with the sequence.

## 11. VFX direction

VFX must explain state first and decorate second. Effects are strongest when they are local, brief, and causally attached to the interaction.

### Feedback hierarchy

| Event | Visual treatment | Target feel |
|---|---|---|
| Tap down | 4% pad compression, tighter highlight, 1–2 px rim spike, compact radial ripple | Immediate and physical |
| Correct step | Local identity-colored pulse, then clean return | Confident; never celebratory on every tap |
| Sequence complete | One perimeter sweep and resolved four-note-compatible chord | Relief and handoff readiness |
| Twist reveal | Card snap plus one perimeter motion that demonstrates the rule | Tactical and legible |
| Turn handoff | Orange/cyan ownership sweep; old owner dims as new owner rises | Social baton pass |
| Wrong step | Brief freeze, error outline, short fracture pulse, then expected-step ghost | Clear diagnosis, not punishment |
| Rally point | Crown/pip arrival with short burst | Rewarding but rematch-safe |
| Match win | Larger crown and controlled confetti, 2–3 seconds and skippable | Memorable climax |

### Timing targets

- touch response begins within one rendered frame where practical;
- pad press-in: 50–70 ms;
- pad release: 90–130 ms;
- local correct pulse: 100–160 ms;
- Twist reveal: about 650 ms after first encounter; up to 1.25 s with first-use explanation;
- handoff sweep: 350–500 ms;
- failure diagnosis: 650–900 ms before the point celebration;
- match celebration: 2–3 seconds, skippable;
- no nonessential ambient movement during sequence playback or recall.

Reduced Motion replaces orbiting, scale-heavy, and camera-like transitions with opacity, rim, and positional markers.

## 12. Sound and music direction

Sound is part of the memory system, not a decoration layer.

### Pad identities

Choose four notes from a consonant voicing so arbitrary sequences remain pleasant. A strong prototype is **E4, G4, B4, E5**: an E-minor voicing in a phone-friendly midrange. Add distinguishing harmonics and attacks rather than relying on pure sine tones, then validate recognition on phone speakers.

| Pad | Pitch region | Timbre direction | Envelope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circle / coral | E4 / 329.6 Hz | rounded mallet or soft synth body | 5–10 ms attack, 160–220 ms body, release at most 80 ms |
| Square / mint | G4 / 392.0 Hz | warm, woody pluck | clear onset, 160–220 ms body |
| Triangle / sun | B4 / 493.9 Hz | bright bell/pluck with controlled high end | crisp onset, 160–200 ms body |
| Star / electric | E5 / 659.3 Hz | clean glassy synth without piercing resonance | crisp onset, 160–220 ms body |

Prototype note and timbre together. Pitch alone is not enough on small speakers or in noisy rooms.

### Event audio

- Playback and player input use the exact same pad sounds.
- Correct sequence ends with a quiet resolved stinger, not applause.
- Wrong input interrupts immediately with a short low-information error transient, then replays the expected identity.
- Reverse, Shift, and Echo each receive a short recognizable earcon whose rhythm mirrors the rule.
- Player 1 and Player 2 use short orange/cyan ownership motifs separated by register or instrumentation, not louder volume.
- Turn, rally, and match celebrations scale in musical size.
- Duck or stop background music during playback and recall.
- Reserve adaptive tension beds for final rally, long sequence, and results; do not compete with memory tones.

### Mix and settings

- Separate Music, SFX, Voice, and Haptics controls where voice is used.
- Test on phone speakers first, then inexpensive earbuds and Bluetooth devices.
- Avoid important sub-bass below what phone speakers reproduce.
- Keep pad sounds short enough that fast playback remains intelligible.
- Use Learning playback at 420 ms cue + 140 ms gap, Standard at 320 + 120 ms, and Fast at 250 + 100 ms as prototype starting points.
- Keep at least -1 dB true-peak headroom and test mono playback.
- Add an audiovisual calibration test before considering rhythm-dependent modes.
- Provide visual-only and audio-only practice so each modality is genuinely usable.

## 13. Haptic direction

Use haptics as a crisp causal confirmation. Continuous buzzing quickly becomes annoying and numbing.

| Event | Haptic pattern |
|---|---|
| Pad tap | very short light transient, consistent per valid touch |
| Sequence complete | light-medium resolved transient or compact doublet |
| Twist armed | single sharper transient |
| Shift complete | two low-intensity ticks aligned to settle points, not a continuous vibration |
| Wrong input | one short heavier transient, never a long buzz |
| Rally point | compact two-beat pattern |
| Match win | short three-beat pattern, optional and skippable with celebration |

Rules:

- visual, audio, and haptic peaks must align;
- no haptic should be required to understand normal play;
- avoid vibration during every playback step unless the player enables haptic playback;
- respect OS settings and include a game-level toggle;
- do not use haptics continuously for timers;
- validate on representative iOS and Android hardware because actuator quality varies widely.

Sources: [Apple HIG — Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics), [Android haptics principles](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/haptics/haptics-principles).

## 14. Accessibility and comfort

The game should never rely on color alone.

### Required redundancy

- Circle, Triangle, Square, and Star keep distinct shape, symbol, color, tone, and optional haptic identity.
- Player ownership uses labels, crowns, placement, and orange/cyan—not color alone.
- Player 1 uses a solid perimeter pattern; Player 2 uses a segmented perimeter pattern.
- Error uses X/fracture/outline language so it cannot be confused with the coral pad.
- Active Twist is named in text and shown by motion or positional markers.
- Captions identify voice or important sound-only state changes.

### Settings and behavior

- Reduced Motion: replace orbit/zoom/shake with fades, outlines, and position markers.
- High Contrast: opaque panels, stronger borders, reduced glass and bloom.
- Playback Speed: relaxed, standard, fast; never hide basic rules behind speed.
- Input Time: standard, generous, unlimited in practice.
- Visual-only and audio-only practice.
- Independent music, SFX, voice, and haptic controls.
- Large touch targets; keep core controls well above the platform 44 pt recommendation.
- Avoid sustained flashing; do not use more than three high-contrast flashes per second.
- Pause and obscure the sequence when the app loses focus.

Sources: [Apple HIG — Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Apple HIG — Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion), [Apple — Differentiate Without Color Alone](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/differentiate-without-color-alone-evaluation-criteria), [Apple HIG — Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls).

### Current-palette contrast corrections

The current palette produces approximately 2.95:1 for white on coral and 2.79:1 for white on electric blue, below the 3:1 meaningful non-text target. Use Dark Ink `#07142B` for the coral and blue pad glyphs, or preserve a white fill only with a thick, continuous Dark Ink keyline that stays visible through every glow state. Use `#4679A8` or brighter for interactive strokes on Navy 800, and `#8199B5` or brighter for small secondary text. Test the least-contrasting point of every gradient, not only its base token.

Sources: [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [Understanding Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color), [Understanding Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast).

## 15. Mode roadmap

### First playable

- Pass & Play Duel
- Solo bot fallback

### Version 1.0, only after core validation

- Solo Practice: Classic, Twist Practice, Endless
- Party Elimination for 3–8 local players
- Local names and rivalry history
- Cosmetic board, sound, and player themes
- Daily seeded pattern with friends comparison

### Later exploration

- asynchronous challenge link or result card;
- friend challenges with a deterministic seed;
- online duels only after local retention, latency strategy, reconnection, fair-play, moderation, and service cost are understood.

Do not let networking delay the first social test. Same-phone play is the low-friction differentiator, not a placeholder.

## 16. Retention without bloat

The first return loop should be social, not administrative.

### Core return drivers

- instant Rematch;
- local cup or best-of-series score;
- longest rally and Twist-survival records;
- player-versus-player local history;
- one named Daily Pattern later;
- light cosmetic themes earned through play;
- shareable result card or deterministic challenge link later.

Avoid energy, chores, daily claim calendars, forced accounts, notification spam, or a large economy before the duel itself produces repeat play.

## 17. Monetization recommendation

Recommended launch model:

- complete local duel is free;
- no ad before the first match;
- no ad between turns, during a rally, or before an immediate Rematch;
- optional rewarded ad only after a fully completed match and never attached to competitive advantage;
- one-time **Duel Pass** in the approximate **US$2.99–$4.99** range for ad removal plus cosmetic board, sound, and player themes;
- no subscription until the product genuinely delivers recurring content;
- no energy, paid revive, Twist power, stat boost, or guest disadvantage.

Test price and ad policy by region. Trust is part of the local party-game experience: one player's purchase should not make the other person's match worse.

## 18. Measurement plan

### First-session funnel

Track:

1. launch;
2. mode selected;
3. first interactive input;
4. first normal turn completed by both players;
5. first Twist seen;
6. first Twist survived or failed;
7. first match completed;
8. Rematch selected;
9. second match completed.

### Core metrics

| Metric | Prototype target | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Launch to first input | median under 20 s | Measures setup friction |
| Normal-turn comprehension | at least 85% without verbal rescue | Proves the loop teaches itself |
| Twist comprehension | at least 80% can explain the rule before input | Protects fairness |
| First-match completion | at least 80% | Detects pacing and confusion |
| Immediate rematch | at least 50% of completed first matches | Strongest early signal of social fun |
| Median first match | 60–180 s | Fits the intended session |
| Input response | under 80 ms perceived | Protects tactile trust |
| Color-independent success | no meaningful completion gap in color-vision test modes | Validates redundant identity |

These are internal prototype gates, not market benchmarks. Adjust after observing real play.

### Qualitative signals

Observe and code:

- laughing, taunting, blaming, celebrating, leaning in, coaching, or spectating;
- whether players announce or narrate the pattern;
- whether the phone handoff feels natural;
- whether failures feel earned;
- whether a Twist creates a tactical decision or only randomness;
- whether players ask for another match before seeing progression.

## 19. Prototype test sequence

### Test A — paper / facilitator prototype

Use four physical cards or large screen buttons. Manually run Repeat → Add One → Handoff. Test whether authored extension alone produces intent and tension.

### Test B — graybox local build

No progression, store, cosmetics, or online. Add exact tones, touch-down response, score, Rematch, and one Twist token per player. Test 10–15 pairs.

### Test C — twist economy comparison

Compare:

- one visible Twist per rally;
- three-charge earned Twist;
- random bag versus player choice.

Choose the smallest system that creates a meaningful timing decision.

### Test D — sensory polish vertical slice

Implement final-quality touch response, four pad sounds, one handoff, one failure, and one match win. Test at arm's length, in a mildly noisy room, and with sound or color disabled.

### Test E — store-message test

Show a 10–15 second silent capture to unbriefed viewers. They should understand that two people are challenging each other, one adds to the pattern, and Twists change the next turn.

## 20. Key risks and gates

| Risk | Why it matters | Gate / mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Working title and trade dress | Simon is a Hasbro brand; historical Simon products include Reverse, Pass It, alternating two-player play, and other variants. | Keep “Simon Duel” internal, obtain professional name/trademark/trade-dress review before public branding, and avoid the classic circular four-quadrant silhouette. |
| Twist novelty | Reverse and Shuffle already exist in multiple products. | Sell player-authored tactical sabotage and social theater, not a feature checklist. Rename Shuffle to Shift and validate timing agency. |
| Low content depth | One mechanic may feel exhausted. | Prove rematch and rivalry first; add Daily, Party, bot, and cosmetics only against observed needs. |
| Sensory overload | Glow, music, particles, and haptics can impair recall. | Memory phase has the strictest sensory budget; gameplay signal always wins. |
| Ad damage | Ads can interrupt the precise social momentum that differentiates the game. | Never place ads in rallies, handoffs, or immediate rematches. |
| Online scope | Networking adds setup, latency, reconnection, moderation, service, and entitlement complexity. | Defer until the local loop is proven. |
| Cognitive claims | Unsupported benefit claims create legal and trust risk. | Describe play and pattern recall only; do not promise intelligence or health outcomes. |

This section is a product-risk observation, not legal advice.

## 21. Decision checklist

Approve now:

- social-toy positioning;
- one-phone Pass & Play as the primary mode;
- player-authored Add One loop;
- three-Twist maximum for public prototype;
- current tactile navy/orange/cyan art direction;
- rhythm-grade audio/touch synchronization;
- accessibility as an identity-system requirement;
- free core plus optional one-time cosmetic/ad-removal pass;
- rename and legal-clearance gate before public launch.

Do not approve yet:

- online multiplayer;
- subscription;
- large progression or economy;
- additional Twist catalog;
- final public name;
- public cognitive-benefit claims.
